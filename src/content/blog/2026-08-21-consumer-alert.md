## How do I protect myself after a data breach?

You protect yourself after a data breach by freezing your credit first, because it is free at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion and blocks new-account fraud, described as the "Fort Knox of credit protection" by credit expert John Ulzheimer per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html). Then set fraud alerts and change passwords, as [Michigan AG](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/08/05/ag-nessel-reissues-consumer-alert-on-data-breaches) advises.

## How bad were H1 2026 data breaches, really?

H1 2026 data breaches were severe: 1,803 compromises versus 1,732 in H1 2025, per the [ITRC](https://www.idtheftcenter.org/podcast/weekly-breach-breakdown-itrc-h1-2026-data-breach-report/). The 471.2 million victim notices in H1 2026 already eclipsed the 297.5 million notices for all of 2025, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html) reports. Full-year 2025 saw 3,321 total incidents, so the compromise count did not beat last year's total — only the notices did.

## What is the story behind the 275 million notices from one breach?

The 275 million notices came from a single breach of the education tool Canvas, accounting for more than half of all H1 2026 victim notices, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html). That one incident dwarfed every other reported breach in the period and shows how a single compromised platform can flood the notification system.

## How much are AI-powered attacks driving this?

AI-enabled attacks are a growing share of breaches: one in four breaches was AI-enabled between March 2025 and February 2026, up 56% from a year earlier, per the [IBM Cost of a Data Breach report](https://www.ibm.com/reports/data-breach). These are AI-enabled attacks, not attacks caused by AI, and IBM's numbers are separate from the ITRC incident counts.

## Why are insiders and fake remote workers a new threat?

Malicious insider incidents jumped to 21 in H1 2026 from just 3 in all of 2025, a 7x increase, per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html). Drivers include disgruntled laid-off employees and North Korea placing remote IT workers in US businesses using stolen identities, deepfake interview videos, and AI-generated resumes, as flagged by the [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2025/north-korean-it-worker-threats-to-u-s-businesses). The deepfake angle also reaches consumers directly, as we covered in [our guide to AI voice-clone scams](https://cybersec-ai.xyz/blog/2026-07-24-consumer-alert/).

## Why do some breach notices tell you almost nothing?

Only 24% of consumer breach notices in H1 2026 disclosed what data was exposed, down from 93% in 2021, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html) reports. As ITRC president James Lee put it, "where you live determines if you find out" — meaning disclosure rules vary by jurisdiction, and some consumers get vague notices while others get specifics.

## What should I do right now to protect my accounts and credit?

Take these six steps immediately to lock down your financial life.

1. **Freeze your credit** at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. It is free and described as the "Fort Knox of credit protection" per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html).
2. **Pull free weekly credit reports** at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/), the federally authorized source, per [Michigan AG](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/08/05/ag-nessel-reissues-consumer-alert-on-data-breaches).
3. **Place a fraud alert** on your file — it is free, lasts one year, and any bureau forwards it to the others, [Michigan AG](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/08/05/ag-nessel-reissues-consumer-alert-on-data-breaches) notes.
4. **Turn on MFA** (multi-factor authentication) for email, banking, and financial apps, per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html).
5. **Change passwords**, especially reused ones, and use a password manager, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html) advises.
6. **Watch for phishing** texts and emails exploiting stolen data — see [our alert on fake package-delivery texts](https://cybersec-ai.xyz/blog/2026-08-14-consumer-alert/).

## FAQ

### How do I freeze my credit?

You freeze your credit by visiting each bureau's freeze page — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — and requesting a freeze, which is free and does not hurt your credit score, per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html). You will receive a PIN to lift the freeze later, [Michigan AG](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/08/05/ag-nessel-reissues-consumer-alert-on-data-breaches) explains.

### Do credit freezes cost money?

No, credit freezes are free at all three bureaus, per [Michigan AG](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/08/05/ag-nessel-reissues-consumer-alert-on-data-breaches). A freeze blocks new-account credit pulls, but it does not affect existing accounts or your credit score, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html) confirms.

### What if my data was already exposed in a breach?

If your data was already exposed, freeze your credit first, then monitor your statements and pull weekly credit reports, per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/14/data-breaches-surge-2026-ai-cyberattacks.html) and [Michigan AG](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/news/press-releases/2026/08/05/ag-nessel-reissues-consumer-alert-on-data-breaches).
